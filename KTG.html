Introduction, Ideal Gas & Assumptions of KTG

Pages 1 – 2
🎯 Core Objective of KTG

In Kinetic Theory of Gases (KTG), we relate macroscopic properties of a gas (like Pressure $P$, Temperature $T$, Volume $V$) to the microscopic random motion of its molecules.

✨ Ideal Gas: Two Mandatory Conditions

Condition 1: No Intermolecular Forces

$F_{\text{intermolecular}} = 0 \implies$ Since there are no intermolecular attractive/repulsive forces between molecules of an ideal gas, there is zero potential energy ($\text{PE} = 0$) corresponding to it.

Condition 2: Point Mass Volume ($V_{\text{gas}} \gg V_{\text{molecules}}$)

Volume occupied by the gas (container volume $V$) is much larger than the actual volume of the gas molecules $\implies$ Separation between molecules $\gg$ size of molecules.

🔥
When do Real Gases behave like Ideal Gases?
Under High Temperature & Low Pressure (low density $\implies$ large intermolecular separation & negligible intermolecular forces).

📌 The 6 Fundamental Assumptions of KTG

Pages 1 & 2
1
Rigid Elastic Spheres

Gas molecules are rigid, perfectly elastic spheres of very small diameters, and they obey Newton's laws of motion.

2
Identical Molecules

All the molecules of a given gas are completely identical in shape, size, and mass ($m$).

3
Molecular Chaos

Molecules are consistently in a state of random motion moving in all possible directions with different velocities — called Molecular Chaos.

4
Elastic Collisions (No Energy Loss)

Molecules continuously collide with each other & with container walls. All collisions are perfectly elastic (no loss of kinetic energy).

5
Force-Free Straight Line Paths

Molecules do not exert any force on each other (except during collision) and hence travel in straight lines between two successive collisions.

6
Negligible Collision Duration

The time required for a collision itself is very small ($\ll$) compared to the time interval between two successive collisions (relaxation time).

Kinetic Gas Equation & Translational Kinetic Energy

Pages 2 – 4, 12 – 13
Cube of side $l$, Area $A=l^2$, $V=l^3$
Sample Parameters (Page 2)
$N$ : Total molecules
$N_A$ : Avogadro's no.
$n$ : No. of moles
$m$ : Mass of 1 molecule
$M$ : Mass of sample ($mN$)
$M_0$ : Molar mass ($mN_A$)

A molecule of mass $m$ collides elastically with the shaded wall perpendicular to the $X$-axis ($v_x$ reverses to $-v_x$, while $v_y, v_z$ remain unchanged).

Step-by-Step Derivation (Pages 2–3)
  1. Momentum transferred to wall per collision: $\Delta P = 2 m v_x$
  2. Time between two successive collisions with same wall: $\Delta t = \dfrac{2l}{v_x}$
  3. Average force exerted by 1 molecule on wall: $f = \dfrac{\Delta P}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{2 m v_x}{2l / v_x} = \dfrac{m v_x^2}{l}$
  4. Total force exerted on wall by all $N$ molecules: $F = \sum f = \sum \left(\dfrac{m}{l} v_x^2\right) = \dfrac{m}{l} \sum v_x^2$
  5. Pressure exerted on wall ($A = l^2, V = l^3$):
    $$P = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{F}{l^2} = \frac{m}{l^3} \sum v_x^2 = \frac{m}{V} \sum v_x^2 \implies PV = m \sum v_x^2 \quad \text{--- (Eq. 1)}$$
  6. By Isotropy of Random Motion: $\sum v_x^2 = \sum v_y^2 = \sum v_z^2$. Since $v^2 = v_x^2 + v_y^2 + v_z^2$:
    $$\sum v^2 = \sum (v_x^2 + v_y^2 + v_z^2) = 3 \sum v_x^2 \implies \sum v_x^2 = \frac{1}{3} \sum v^2$$
  7. Substituting in Eq. (1) & introducing $v_{\text{rms}}^2 = \frac{\sum v^2}{N}$:
    $$PV = \frac{1}{3} m \sum v^2 = \frac{1}{3} (mN) \left(\frac{\sum v^2}{N}\right) = \frac{1}{3} M v_{\text{rms}}^2$$

⚡ Master Equations: Kinetic Gas Equation

$$PV = \frac{1}{3} m N v_{\text{rms}}^2 = \frac{1}{3} M v_{\text{rms}}^2$$
$$P = \frac{1}{3} \frac{M}{V} v_{\text{rms}}^2 = \frac{1}{3} \rho v_{\text{rms}}^2 \quad \left(\rho = \frac{M}{V} = \text{density}\right)$$
Definition of $v_{\text{rms}}$: Root Mean Square speed is the square root of the mean/average of squares of speeds of all gas molecules: $$v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum v^2}{N}} = \sqrt{\frac{v_1^2 + v_2^2 + \dots + v_N^2}{N}}$$

🔋 Translational Kinetic Energy ($K$)

Pages 3, 12–13

Since $K = \sum \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{1}{2} m \sum v^2 = \frac{1}{2} m (N v_{\text{rms}}^2) = \frac{1}{2} M v_{\text{rms}}^2$:

$$PV = \frac{2}{3} \left(\frac{1}{2} M v_{\text{rms}}^2\right) \implies \mathbf{PV = \frac{2}{3} K} \quad \text{or} \quad \mathbf{K = \frac{3}{2} PV}$$
1. Total Sample ($n$ moles)
Using $v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{3RT/M_0}$
$K = \frac{1}{2} M v_{\text{rms}}^2 = \frac{3}{2} nRT = \frac{3}{2} PV$
2. For $1\text{ Mole}$ of Gas ($K_1$)
$K_1 = K / n$ (Only depends on $T$!)
$K_1 = \frac{3}{2} RT$
3. Average KE of $1\text{ Molecule}$
$K_{\text{molecule}} = K_1 / N_A$ (Only depends on $T$!)
$K_{\text{mol}} = \frac{3}{2} k_B T$
💡 Note from Page 12 & 13: Translational KE of $1\text{ mole}$ ($\frac{3}{2}RT$) and $1\text{ molecule}$ ($\frac{3}{2}k_B T$) depends ONLY on absolute temperature $T$ (independent of nature/molar mass/atomicity of gas).

Ideal Gas Equation & The 4 Classical Gas Laws

Pages 4 – 6
🔗 Derivation of Ideal Gas Equation from KTG (Page 4)

From the kinetic gas equation: $PV = \frac{1}{3} m N v_{\text{rms}}^2 = \frac{2}{3} N \left(\frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{rms}}^2\right)$. Here $\left(\frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{rms}}^2\right)$ represents the average translational kinetic energy of a single gas molecule. As per the definition of the absolute temperature scale, random motion of molecules & hence average KE of each molecule is directly proportional to absolute temperature $T$:

$$\left(\frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{rms}}^2\right) = C T \implies PV = \frac{2}{3} N (CT) = N \left(\frac{2}{3} C\right) T \implies \mathbf{PV = N k_B T}$$ $$\text{Since } N = n N_A \implies PV = n (N_A k_B) T \implies \mathbf{PV = nRT} \quad (\text{where } R = N_A k_B)$$
Molecular Form
$$PV = N k_B T$$
$k_B = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}\text{ J/K}$
Molar Form
$$PV = n R T$$
$R = 8.31 \approx \frac{25}{3}\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K}$
Density Form (Page 6)
$$\rho = \frac{P M_0}{R T}$$
Since $n = \frac{M}{M_0}$ and $\rho = \frac{M}{V}$
Number Density ($\rho_n$)
$$\rho_n = \frac{N}{V} = \frac{P}{k_B T}$$
Molecules per unit volume
1. Boyle's Law $T, n = \text{const}$

For an enclosed gas at constant temperature, product of $P$ and $V$ remains constant:

$PV = \text{const} \implies P_i V_i = P_f V_f$
Curve: Rectangular Hyperbola
2. Charles's Law $P, n = \text{const}$

For an enclosed gas at constant pressure, volume is directly proportional to temperature:

$V \propto T \implies \dfrac{V_i}{T_i} = \dfrac{V_f}{T_f}$
Straight line through origin
3. Gay-Lussac's Law $V, n = \text{const}$

For an enclosed gas at constant volume, pressure is directly proportional to temperature:

$P \propto T \implies \dfrac{P_i}{T_i} = \dfrac{P_f}{T_f}$
Straight line through origin
4. Avogadro's Law $P, T = \text{const}$

At same $P$ & $T$, equal volumes of all ideal gases contain the same number of molecules:

$V \propto N \quad (\text{or } V \propto n)$
1 mol @ STP = 22.4 L

Molecular Speeds ($v_{\text{mp}}, v_{\text{avg}}, v_{\text{rms}}$) & Maxwell's Distribution Law

Pages 7 – 10

🚀 Complete Comparison of Molecular Speeds

Order: $v_{\text{rms}} > v_{\text{avg}} > v_{\text{mp}}$  |  Ratio: $\sqrt{3} : \sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}} : \sqrt{2} \approx 1.732 : 1.596 : 1.414$
Speed Type Discrete & Calculus Definition Molar Form ($R, M_0$) Molecular Form ($k_B, m$) Pressure-Density Form
1. Most Probable Speed ($v_{\text{mp}}$)
Modal speed possessed by max molecules; $\frac{d}{dv}\left(\frac{dN}{dv}\right) = 0$
 Peak of $\dfrac{dN}{dv}$ curve $$\sqrt{\frac{2RT}{M_0}}$$ $$\sqrt{\frac{2k_B T}{m}}$$ $$\sqrt{\frac{2P}{\rho}}$$
2. Average Speed ($v_{\text{avg}}$)
Arithmetic mean of speeds of all molecules
 $$\frac{v_1+\dots+v_N}{N} = \frac{\sum v_i}{N} = \frac{\int v\,dN}{N} = \frac{\int v f\,dv}{N}$$ $$\sqrt{\frac{8RT}{\pi M_0}}$$ $$\sqrt{\frac{8k_B T}{\pi m}}$$ $$\sqrt{\frac{8P}{\pi \rho}}$$
3. RMS Speed ($v_{\text{rms}}$)
Root of average of squares of speeds
 $$\sqrt{\frac{v_1^2+\dots+v_N^2}{N}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum v_i^2}{N}} = \sqrt{\langle v^2 \rangle} = \sqrt{\frac{\int v^2\,dN}{N}}$$ $$\sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M_0}}$$ $$\sqrt{\frac{3k_B T}{m}}$$ $$\sqrt{\frac{3P}{\rho}}$$
⚠️ High-Yield Concept Trap: Vector Velocity/Momentum vs. Scalar Speed (Page 7 Notes)
1. Average Velocity (Sample)
$\langle \vec{v} \rangle = \vec{0}$

Because molecules move randomly in all possible directions.

2. Avg Velocity (1 Molecule)
$\langle \vec{v}_{\text{single}} \rangle = \vec{0}$

Over a long period of time, net displacement/velocity is zero.

3. Average Speed of Gas
$v_{\text{avg}} = \langle |\vec{v}| \rangle \neq 0$

Speed is always positive ($v_{\text{avg}} = \sqrt{8RT/\pi M_0}$).

4. Average Momentum
$\langle \vec{P} \rangle = \vec{0}$  but  $\langle |\vec{P}| \rangle \neq 0$

Avg of magnitude of momentum: $\langle |\vec{P}| \rangle = m v_{\text{avg}} \neq 0$.

Mean Free Path ($\lambda$) & Relaxation Time ($\tau$)

Pages 10 – 12
1. Mean Free Path ($\lambda$)

Average distance covered by a molecule between two successive collisions:

$$\lambda = \frac{L_1 + L_2 + \dots + L_N}{N}$$
2. Relaxation Time ($\tau$)

Average time spent by a molecule between two successive collisions:

$$\tau = \frac{t_1 + t_2 + \dots + t_N}{N}$$
Collision Cylinder (Page 11)

A molecule of diameter $d$ collides with any other molecule whose center lies within a cylinder of radius $d$ (cross-sectional area $A = \pi d^2$) and length $l = v_{\text{avg}}\Delta t$.

Derivation & Relative Velocity Correction (Pages 11–12)
  1. Number Density: $\rho_n = \dfrac{N}{V} \implies N = \rho_n V$ (molecules per unit volume).
  2. Volume swept in $\Delta t$: $V_{\text{cyl}} = (\pi d^2) l = \pi d^2 v_{\text{avg}} \Delta t$.
  3. No. of collisions in $\Delta t$: $N_{\text{coll}} = \rho_n V_{\text{cyl}} = \rho_n \pi d^2 v_{\text{avg}} \Delta t$.
  4. Collision Frequency ($f$): $f = \dfrac{N_{\text{coll}}}{\Delta t} = \rho_n \pi d^2 v_{\text{avg}} \implies \tau_{\text{uncorrected}} = \dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{\rho_n \pi d^2 v_{\text{avg}}}$.
  5. Relative Motion Correction (Page 12): Since target molecules are also moving, replace $v_{\text{avg}}$ with average relative velocity $\mathbf{(v_{\text{rel}})_{\text{avg}} = \sqrt{2}\,v_{\text{avg}}}$!

⚡ Corrected Master Equations (Page 12)

1. Fundamental Relation:
$$\lambda = v_{\text{avg}} \cdot \tau$$
2. Corrected Relaxation Time ($\tau$):
$$\tau = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\rho_n\,d^2\,v_{\text{avg}}}$$
3. Mean Free Path ($\lambda$) — Density & $P, T$ Forms:
$$\lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\rho_n\,d^2} = \frac{k_B T}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,d^2\,P}$$
Since $PV = N k_B T \implies \rho_n = \dfrac{N}{V} = \dfrac{P}{k_B T}$
📌 Proportionalities to Remember
• $\lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{d^2}$ (molecular size)
• $\lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{\rho_n}$ (number density)
• At const $P$: $\lambda \propto T$
• At const $T$: $\lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{P}$
• In a rigid sealed container ($N, V$ const): $\lambda$ is independent of $T$ and $P$!

Degrees of Freedom ($f$), Equipartition of Energy & Internal Energy ($U$)

Pages 13 – 16

🎯 Degrees of Freedom (D.F. or $f$) — Dual Definition (Page 13)

1. Number of independent modes in which a given gas molecule can possess energy.
2. Number of independent squared terms in the expression for energy of a given molecule.

Atomicity / Gas Type Trans ($f_t$) Rot ($f_r$) Total ($f$) Energy Expression & Reason (Pages 13–14)
Monoatomic
($\text{He, Ne, Ar}$)		 3 0 3 $K = \frac{1}{2}mv_x^2 + \frac{1}{2}mv_y^2 + \frac{1}{2}mv_z^2$ (Point mass $\implies I=0$)
Diatomic / Linear Polyatomic
($\text{H}_2, \text{O}_2, \text{N}_2$)		 3 2 5 Along bond axis $I_x = 0$, while $I_y \neq 0, I_z \neq 0$:
$K = (\frac{1}{2}mv_x^2 + \frac{1}{2}mv_y^2 + \frac{1}{2}mv_z^2) + (\frac{1}{2}I_y\omega_y^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_z\omega_z^2)$
Non-Linear Polyatomic
($\text{CH}_4, \text{NH}_3, \text{SO}_2$)		 3 3 6 Moment of inertia is non-zero along all 3 axes ($I_x, I_y, I_z \neq 0$).
〰️ Vibrational Degrees of Freedom (Pages 14–15) 1 Mode = 2 DOF

Chemical bonds in diatomic/polyatomic molecules are modeled mathematically like a spring ($\text{O}\!-\!\text{www}\!-\!\text{O}$). Vibrating atoms possess both kinetic & potential energy:

$$E_{\text{vib}} = \text{KE} + \text{PE} = \frac{1}{2}\mu v_{\text{rel}}^2 + \frac{1}{2}kr^2 \implies \mathbf{2\text{ D.O.F. per mode of vibration (1 for KE + 1 for PE)}}$$
When to count? Effective only at higher temperatures; not considered unless explicitly stated in the question.
🚨 $\text{CO}_2$ Exception (Page 15): $\text{CO}_2$ is an exception — in $\text{CO}_2$, vibrational degrees of freedom are activated even at room temperature!

⚖️ Law of Equipartition of Energy

Page 15

The average energy possessed by a molecule at temperature $T$ is distributed equally in each degree of freedom:

1. Avg energy of 1 molecule per DOF: $\dfrac{1}{2} k_B T$
2. Total avg energy of 1 molecule ($f$ DOF): $\dfrac{f}{2} k_B T$
3. Total energy of 1 mole ($N_A \cdot \frac{f}{2}k_B T$): $\dfrac{f}{2} R T$
4. Total energy of sample ($n$ moles): $\dfrac{f}{2} n R T$

🔥 Internal Energy of Gas ($U$ or $\text{IE}$)

Pages 15–16

Internal energy of a gas is the energy possessed by it due to random motion of its molecules & PE due to intermolecular attractions:

General Real Gas:
$\text{IE} = \underbrace{\left(\text{Trans KE} + \text{Rot KE} + \text{Vib KE} + \text{Vib PE}\right)}_{\text{Energy due to random motion}} + \text{PE}_{\text{intermolecular attractions}}$
For an Ideal Gas ($\text{PE}_{\text{intermolecular}} = 0$):
$$U = \frac{f}{2} nRT \quad \text{and} \quad \Delta U = \frac{f}{2} nR\Delta T$$

Dulong-Petit Law (Crystalline Solids) & Specific Heat of Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)

Pages 16 – 17

💎 Dulong-Petit Law (Crystalline Solids)

$f = 6 \implies C = 3R$

Statement: According to this law, the molar specific heat capacity of crystalline solids at room/higher temperatures is equal to $\mathbf{3R}$.
(Molar specific heat capacity $C = \frac{Q}{n\Delta T}$ is the heat required for unit rise in temperature of $1\text{ mole}$ of substance).

Complete Proof (Page 16):
  • In solids, translational & rotational motions are almost absent $\implies$ only vibrational degrees of freedom are significant.
  • Each solid atom has $3$ modes of vibration (along $x, y, z$ axes).
  • Since there are $2$ DOF corresponding to each mode of vibration ($1$ KE + $1$ PE), total DOF per solid molecule/atom is $\mathbf{f = 3 \times 2 = 6}$.
  • Internal energy of solid: $U = \dfrac{f}{2} nRT = \dfrac{6}{2} nRT = \mathbf{3nRT}$.
  • Heat supplied during heating: $Q = \Delta U = 3nR\Delta T$.
$$C = \frac{Q}{n\Delta T} = \frac{3nR\Delta T}{n\Delta T} = \mathbf{3R} \quad \Bigg| \quad \text{Specific Heat per unit mass: } \mathbf{S = \frac{C}{M_0} = \frac{3R}{M_0}}$$

💧 Molar & Specific Heat Capacity of Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)

$f = 18 \implies C = 9R$

In liquid water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), rotational & translational energies are insignificant and only vibrational degrees of freedom are significant.

Complete Derivation (Page 17):
  • Just like solids, each atom has $6$ vibrational degrees of freedom ($3\text{ modes} \times 2$).
  • Since a water molecule ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) has $3$ atoms ($2\text{ H} + 1\text{ O}$), it has a total of $\mathbf{f = 3 \times 6 = 18}$ degrees of freedom!
  • Internal energy: $U = \dfrac{f}{2} nRT = \dfrac{18}{2} nRT = \mathbf{9nRT} \implies Q = \Delta U = 9nR\Delta T$.
  • Molar Heat Capacity of Water: $C = \dfrac{Q}{n\Delta T} = \dfrac{9nR\Delta T}{n\Delta T} = \mathbf{9R}$.
$$S = \frac{C}{M_0} = \frac{9R}{M_0} = \frac{9 \times (2\text{ cal/mol}\cdot\text{K})}{18\text{ g/mol}} = \mathbf{1\text{ cal/g}\cdot\text{K}}$$

All 6 Solved Problems from Notebook (Step-by-Step)

Pages 6 & 10
Problem 1 • Page 6 Density of Ideal Gas

Q. Find the density ($\rho$) of a gas having molar mass $M_0$ at pressure $P$ and temperature $T$.

• From Ideal Gas Equation: $PV = nRT$
• Substituting number of moles $n = \dfrac{M}{M_0}$:
$$PV = \frac{M}{M_0} RT \implies \frac{P M_0}{RT} = \frac{M}{V}$$
$$\text{Answer: } \rho = \frac{M}{V} = \frac{P M_0}{RT}$$
Problem 2 • Page 6 Percentage Change in $T$

Q. Pressure of an ideal gas is increased by $20\%$ while its volume is increased by $40\%$. Find the percentage change in temperature of the gas.

• Initial state: $PV = nRT$
• New Pressure: $P' = 1.2 P$ ($+20\%$)  |  New Volume: $V' = 1.4 V$ ($+40\%$)
• $(1.2 P)(1.4 V) = n R T' \implies 1.68 (PV) = n R T'$
• Comparing with $PV = nRT$: $T' = 1.68 T$
$$\text{Answer: } \% \text{ Change in Temperature} = +68\%$$
Problem 3 • Page 6 Gas Mixture Stoichiometry

Q. A $28\text{ g}$ mixture of Neon ($\text{Ne} = 20$) and Argon ($\text{Ar} = 40$) occupies $30\text{ Litre}$ at $10^5\text{ Pa}$ and $300\text{ K}$. Find the mass of Neon in the mixture.

• Let mass of Neon $= x\text{ g} \implies$ mass of Argon $= (28 - x)\text{ g}$.
• Total moles $n = n_{\text{Ne}} + n_{\text{Ar}} = \dfrac{x}{20} + \dfrac{28 - x}{40}$.
• Applying $PV = nRT$ with $V = 30 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3$ and $R = \dfrac{25}{3}\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K}$:
$$10^5 \times 30 \times 10^{-3} = \left(\frac{x}{20} + \frac{28 - x}{40}\right) \times \frac{25}{3} \times 300$$
$$3000 = \left(\frac{2x + 28 - x}{40}\right) \times 2500 \implies \frac{x + 28}{40} = \frac{30}{25} = 1.2 \implies x + 28 = 48$$
$$\text{Answer: Mass of Neon } (x) = 20\text{ g} \quad (\text{and Argon} = 8\text{ g})$$
Problem 4 • Page 6 (Classic JEE SHM) Isothermal Piston Oscillation

Q. A vertical cylinder with a movable piston (mass $m$, area $A$, equilibrium gas height $l$) is in equilibrium. It is pushed downward slightly by $x$ and released. Find the angular frequency $\omega$ and period of resulting SHM assuming temperature remains constant.

1. Equilibrium Gas Pressure: $\mathbf{P = P_0 + \dfrac{mg}{A}}$
2. Since $T = \text{const} \implies PV = \text{const} \implies \dfrac{\Delta P}{P} + \dfrac{\Delta V}{V} = 0$
3. For small downward displacement $x$: $\Delta V = -Ax$ and $V = Al$: $$\Delta P = -P \frac{\Delta V}{V} = -P \frac{(-Ax)}{Al} = \frac{P}{l} x$$
4. Restoring force: $F_{\text{rest}} = -(\Delta P)A = -\dfrac{PA}{l}x \implies ma = -\dfrac{PA}{l}x \implies a = -\left(\dfrac{PA}{ml}\right)x$
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{PA}{ml}} = \sqrt{\frac{P_0 A + mg}{ml}} \implies T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{ml}{P_0 A + mg}}$$
Problem 5 • Page 10 RMS Speed Calculation

Q. Find the RMS speed of Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$) gas at $127^\circ\text{C}$.

• Given: $T = 127 + 273 = 400\text{ K}$, Molar mass of $\text{H}_2$: $M_0 = 2\text{ g/mol} = 2 \times 10^{-3}\text{ kg/mol}$, $R = \dfrac{25}{3}\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K}$.
$$v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M_0}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 \times \left(\frac{25}{3}\right) \times 400}{2 \times 10^{-3}}} = \sqrt{\frac{25 \times 400 \times 10^3}{2}} = \sqrt{5{,}000{,}000}$$
$$\text{Answer: } v_{\text{rms}} = 1000\sqrt{5}\text{ m/s} = \sqrt{5}\text{ km/s} \approx 2236\text{ m/s}$$
Problem 6 • Page 10 (+ Notebook Verification Note) Equating $v_{\text{rms}}$ & $v_{\text{mp}}$

Q. At what temperature is the RMS speed of Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) at $200^\circ\text{C}$ the same as the Most Probable Speed ($v_{\text{mp}}$) of Helium ($\text{He}$)?

• Given: $T_{\text{O}_2} = 200 + 273 = 473\text{ K}$, $M_{\text{O}_2} = 32 \times 10^{-3}\text{ kg/mol}$, $M_{\text{He}} = 4 \times 10^{-3}\text{ kg/mol}$.
• Step 1 (Written in Notebook): Equating $(v_{\text{rms}})_{\text{O}_2} = (v_{\text{mp}})_{\text{He}}$: $$\sqrt{\frac{3R(473)}{32 \times 10^{-3}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 R T_{\text{He}}}{4 \times 10^{-3}}}$$
✅ Exact Solution for $T_{\text{He}}$:
$$\frac{3 \times 473}{32} = \frac{2 \times T_{\text{He}}}{4}$$ $$T_{\text{He}} = \frac{3 \times 473 \times 4}{32 \times 2} = \frac{1419}{16} = \mathbf{88.69\text{ K}}$$
📝 Notebook Step 2 Note:
In the notebook's 2nd line, $T$ and $473$ were swapped across the fractions ($\frac{3T}{32\times 10^{-3}} = \frac{2\times 473}{4\times 10^{-3}}$), giving: $$T = \frac{32 \times 2 \times 473}{3 \times 4} = \frac{7568}{3} \approx 2522.67\text{ K}$$ (which is $T_{\text{O}_2}$ if $\text{He}$ were at $473\text{ K}$).

📐 KTG 1-Page Rapid Formula & Pocket Revision Sheet

Every equation, constant, ratio, and DOF value from your 17-page notebook in one dense view.

1. Kinetic Gas & Ideal Gas Eqns
• $\Delta P_{\text{wall}} = 2mv_x$,   $f_{\text{1 mol}} = \dfrac{mv_x^2}{l}$
• $PV = \dfrac{1}{3} m N v_{\text{rms}}^2 = \dfrac{1}{3} M v_{\text{rms}}^2$
• $P = \dfrac{1}{3} \rho v_{\text{rms}}^2 \implies P = \dfrac{2}{3} \left(\dfrac{K}{V}\right)$
• $PV = N k_B T = n R T \quad (R = N_A k_B)$
• $\rho = \dfrac{P M_0}{RT}$,   $\rho_n = \dfrac{N}{V} = \dfrac{P}{k_B T}$
2. Translational Kinetic Energy
• $K_{\text{sample}} = \dfrac{1}{2} M v_{\text{rms}}^2 = \dfrac{3}{2} PV = \dfrac{3}{2} nRT$
• $K_{\text{1 mole}} = \dfrac{K}{n} = \dfrac{3}{2} RT$ (Only fn of $T$)
• $K_{\text{1 molecule}} = \dfrac{3}{2} k_B T$ (Only fn of $T$)
• $k_B = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}\text{ J/K}$
• $R = 8.31\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K} \approx \dfrac{25}{3}\text{ J/mol}\cdot\text{K} \approx 2\text{ cal/mol}\cdot\text{K}$
3. Molecular Speeds ($v_{\text{rms}} > v_{\text{avg}} > v_{\text{mp}}$)
• $v_{\text{mp}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2RT}{M_0}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2k_B T}{m}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2P}{\rho}}$
• $v_{\text{avg}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{8RT}{\pi M_0}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{8k_B T}{\pi m}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{8P}{\pi \rho}}$
• $v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}{M_0}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3k_B T}{m}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3P}{\rho}}$
• Ratio: $v_{\text{rms}} : v_{\text{avg}} : v_{\text{mp}} = \sqrt{3} : \sqrt{\dfrac{8}{\pi}} : \sqrt{2}$
• $\langle \vec{v} \rangle = \vec{0},\ \langle \vec{P} \rangle = \vec{0}$, but $\langle |\vec{P}| \rangle = m v_{\text{avg}} \neq 0$
4. Mean Free Path ($\lambda$) & Relaxation ($\tau$)
• $(v_{\text{rel}})_{\text{avg}} = \sqrt{2}\,v_{\text{avg}}$
• $\tau = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi \rho_n d^2 v_{\text{avg}}}$
• $\lambda = v_{\text{avg}} \tau = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi \rho_n d^2} = \dfrac{k_B T}{\sqrt{2} \pi d^2 P}$
• Collision frequency $f = \dfrac{1}{\tau} = \sqrt{2} \pi \rho_n d^2 v_{\text{avg}}$
5. DOF ($f$) & Equipartition of Energy
• Monoatomic: $f = 3\ (3T + 0R)$
• Diatomic / Linear Poly: $f = 5\ (3T + 2R)$
• Non-Linear Polyatomic: $f = 6\ (3T + 3R)$
• Vibrational: $2$ DOF per mode ($1\text{ KE} + 1\text{ PE}$); $\text{CO}_2$ active at room temp!
• Energy: $\dfrac{1}{2}k_B T$ per DOF/molecule; $U_{\text{ideal}} = \dfrac{f}{2}nRT$
6. Solids (Dulong-Petit) & Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)
• Crystalline Solid: $3$ vib modes $\implies f = 6$
• $U_{\text{solid}} = 3nRT \implies C_{\text{solid}} = 3R,\ S = \dfrac{3R}{M_0}$
• Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $3$ atoms): $f = 3 \times 6 = 18$
• $U_{\text{water}} = 9nRT \implies C_{\text{water}} = 9R$
• $S_{\text{water}} = \dfrac{9R}{M_0} = \dfrac{9 \times 2}{18} = 1\text{ cal/g}\cdot\text{K}$
